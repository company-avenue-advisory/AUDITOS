# PART 1 — COMPANY BRAIN RESEARCH

## 1.1 What "institutional intelligence" means, mechanistically

World-class AI-native companies (Palantir, Stripe, Anthropic itself in how it treats internal knowledge, and academically the cognitive-architecture literature going back to Newell's SOAR and Anderson's ACT-R) converge on one structural finding: **institutional memory is not one system — it is several systems with different persistence, different failure modes, and different governance requirements, that must be kept separate or they corrupt each other.** A single vector store or single "knowledge base" is a category error. The taxonomy below is borrowed directly from cognitive science (which built it for human memory) because it turns out to map cleanly onto what an enterprise AI system actually needs, and this mapping is now standard in serious enterprise-agent research (see Part 9).

### Organizational memory — the umbrella category
The sum of everything the organization "knows" that outlives any individual employee, session, or model version. Its purpose is continuity: a new CA joining a firm, or a new model version replacing an old one, should not cause the organization to forget what it has learned. The failure mode when this is absent is what every legacy CA firm already lives with — knowledge trapped in one senior partner's head, lost the day they retire. A Company Brain's entire reason for existing is to make that failure structurally impossible.

### Episodic memory — what happened, once, specifically
A record of a specific event: *this* invoice, from *this* vendor, reconciled on *this* date, with *this* correction applied by *this* reviewer. High volume, high specificity, low reusability on its own. Critically, in a financial/audit context episodic memory has a **legal retention obligation independent of its usefulness for reasoning** — under Indian law, books of account and supporting vouchers must be retained (Companies Act 2013 requires 8 years; GST law requires 72 months from the due date of the annual return; Income Tax Act requires 6 years from the end of the relevant assessment year, longer if transfer-pricing or search-and-seizure provisions apply). This means episodic memory cannot be pruned on a "we don't need this anymore" basis the way a typical ML system's training-data cache can — its retention policy is set by statute, not by relevance decay. This is a structural difference from every generic-AI memory-system design pattern in the literature, and it is domain-specific to finance.

### Semantic memory — what is generally true
Distilled, de-instanced knowledge: "Vendor X's invoices are always issued net-of-discount," "HSN 8471 is commonly misclassified as 8517 by clients in the electronics-trading segment," "Section 43B(h) payment-timeline risk clusters around MSME vendors paid via NEFT with >45 day terms." This is what episodic memory becomes after enough repetition and enough human verification that it can be trusted as a general rule rather than a one-off. The critical discipline (validated by both cognitive-architecture theory and by how Stripe's fraud models actually operate) is that **semantic memory must be provably derived from verified episodic instances, with the derivation traceable** — an unverifiable "the model believes this pattern is true" is not semantic memory, it's an unvalidated hypothesis, and treating it as memory is exactly how hallucination propagates into a system that's supposed to be authoritative.

### Procedural memory — how to do something
Not facts, but executable skill: how to apportion aggregate tax across line items, how to resolve a gross-vs-net parsing ambiguity, how to push a Credit Note as the correct accounting reversal. In a Company Brain, procedural memory should be represented as **executable, deterministic rules wherever the procedure has one provably correct form** (tax math, debit/credit balancing) — this is the same doctrine as WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md's "deterministic before probabilistic," restated at the memory-architecture level: procedural memory for anything with a single correct answer must not be stored as a language-model weight or a soft pattern; it must be stored as code/rules with version history. Only genuinely judgment-based procedures (how to phrase a client query about a discrepancy) belong as soft, LLM-executed procedural memory.

### Trained-in ("weights") memory vs. retrieved memory
A distinction the literature (particularly retrieval-augmented generation research, see Part 9) draws sharply and that matters enormously for a regulated domain: knowledge baked into a model's weights during training is **unversioned, unauditable, and non-retractable** — you cannot prove what a model "knows" from training, you cannot cite where it learned it, and you cannot cleanly remove a single fact if it turns out to be wrong (a stale tax rate, a superseded circular) without retraining. Knowledge that lives in an external, versioned, retrievable store is auditable, retractable, and citable. **The Company Brain doctrine that follows: anything that must be explainable, correctable, or subject to a "this was true until this date" temporal boundary must live in retrieved memory, never rely on trained-in model knowledge.** This is why frontier LLMs, however capable, are structurally the wrong place to store "the current GST rate for HSN X" — not because they're inaccurate today, but because there is no mechanism to prove, audit, retract, or date-bound what they know.

### Knowledge versioning
Every fact in a Company Brain needs, at minimum: an effective-from date, an effective-to date (open-ended if still current), a source citation, a confidence/trust score, and a supersession pointer (what replaced this, if anything). This is not an implementation detail — it is what makes the difference between a system that can honestly answer "was this reconciliation correct according to the rules in force at the time" (the only question that matters to a regulator reviewing a prior-year audit) versus a system that silently applies today's rules retroactively and produces an explainably wrong answer without knowing it's wrong.

### Knowledge freshness and expiration
Freshness is not a single global property — it must be domain-specific (Part 4 works this out per-domain), because a GST rate notification and an ICAI auditing standard decay at wildly different rates. The mechanism that works in practice (used in some form by every serious enterprise-knowledge system, and validated by Anthropic's own internal approach to keeping tool/API knowledge current) is **active expiration with a required re-verification step**, not passive staleness. A fact that hasn't been re-confirmed within its domain-appropriate freshness window should be *flagged as unverified*, not silently trusted and not silently deleted — deletion loses history that may still matter for retroactive/historical reasoning (see episodic memory retention above); silent trust risks acting on stale law.

### Provenance and lineage
Every piece of knowledge, and every downstream conclusion derived from it, needs a traceable lineage back to its origin: which document, which government notification, which reviewer's correction, ingested by which process, on which date, verified by whom. This is what the audit-defensibility instinct from WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md requires at the data layer, not just the output layer — "explain this number" must be answerable not just for a single reconciliation output but for *why the system believed the rule it applied was correct*, all the way back to source.

### Trust scoring
Not every ingested fact deserves equal weight. A trust score should be a function of: source authority (a CBIC notification outranks a tax-consultant blog), verification status (human-confirmed outranks auto-extracted), recency relative to the domain's freshness window, and corroboration (multiple independent sources agreeing). Trust scores should propagate — a conclusion derived from a low-trust fact inherits a capped trust ceiling, and should never be presented to a CA reviewer with false confidence.

### Knowledge validation
Two distinct validation regimes are needed and must not be conflated: **structural validation** (does this fact conform to the expected schema — a tax rate is a percentage, an HSN code is a valid 4/6/8-digit code) is cheap, automatable, and should run on every ingestion. **Semantic/correctness validation** (is this fact actually true) cannot be fully automated for high-stakes domains and requires human verification for anything entering global/domain knowledge layers — this is a direct consequence of the "ground truth is what a licensed professional approved" principle from WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 3, applied upstream to the ingestion pipeline itself, not just to extraction output.

### Conflicting knowledge resolution
Conflicts are not exceptions to be avoided — in a domain with multiple regulators, transitional rules, and jurisdictional overlap (e.g., a central GST notification and a state-specific clarification), conflicts are the *normal* case. The Company Brain needs an explicit conflict-resolution policy, not an assumption they won't occur: (1) more specific always beats more general (a state circular on a specific point beats a general central notification on the same point, for that state); (2) more recent always beats older, *unless* the older rule is still in its effective window for a historical reconciliation being reasoned about (temporal correctness, not "latest wins" naively); (3) higher-authority source beats lower (a CBIC circular beats a professional body's interpretive guidance beats a tax-advisory firm's blog); (4) when a genuine conflict cannot be resolved by these rules, the system must surface the conflict to a human rather than silently picking one side — an unresolved conflict silently defaulted is a worse failure than a system that says "I don't know, a human needs to decide this."

### What should never be stored
Four categories, each for a different reason:
1. **Raw tenant-identifying content in any shared/global knowledge layer** — a specific client's vendor name, GSTIN, or invoice content must never leak into the domain-knowledge or global-knowledge layers that are shared across tenants, even in distilled form, unless explicitly and separately consented to and anonymized. This is the Glean/Stripe lesson from WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md applied as a hard storage-layer rule, not a retrieval-time filter — filtering at retrieval time is not sufficient if the raw data was ever comingled at rest.
2. **Unverified LLM output presented as fact** — an LLM's extraction or inference, before human or deterministic-rule verification, must be stored and clearly tagged as a *proposal*, structurally distinct from verified knowledge, so it can never be silently promoted or accidentally retrieved as ground truth by a future query.
3. **Credentials, secrets, and raw authentication material** — obvious, but worth stating as doctrine given how much of the Company Brain's value depends on ERP/bank/government-portal connectors: connector credentials belong in a secrets system, never in the knowledge store, never in any place an LLM's context window could retrieve them.
4. **Superseded knowledge with no supersession pointer** — deleting an outdated fact without recording what replaced it and when destroys the ability to reason about history (see versioning above); the doctrine is *supersede and retain*, not *overwrite and forget*.

### How obsolete knowledge is removed
Never by silent deletion for anything that was ever globally/domain-trusted knowledge (retain, mark superseded, keep queryable for historical reasoning). Tenant-episodic data follows the statutory retention schedule, not a relevance-based pruning schedule. The only category safely eligible for true deletion is unverified, never-promoted proposals that failed validation and have no downstream dependents — i.e., data that was never trusted in the first place.

## 1.2 How this differs from generic enterprise-AI memory research
Most published "AI agent memory" work (assistant products, coding agents, consumer companions) optimizes for *helpfulness continuity* — remembering user preferences across sessions — and treats staleness/conflict as a minor UX annoyance to be resolved by "most recent wins." Finance is different in kind, not degree: incorrect memory has **statutory liability consequences**, correctness has a **legally defined historical dimension** (what was true on a past date matters as much as what's true now), and the retention/deletion rules are **externally imposed by law**, not internally optimized for cost or relevance. Any Company Brain design imported wholesale from consumer-agent-memory literature will get all three of these wrong by default.

# PART 2 — FINANCIAL ONTOLOGY RESEARCH

## 2.1 Why ontologies, and why not just embeddings

A pure vector/embedding retrieval system answers "what is semantically similar to this query." It cannot natively answer "what does this vendor legally owe," "which ledger entries are related-party transactions requiring disclosure," or "did this reconciliation ever break the accounting equation." Those are **structural, relational, rule-governed questions**, not similarity questions. This is precisely the finding that drove Palantir's founding technical bet (per WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 1): an ontology is a formal, typed model of real-world entities and their relationships, over which *deterministic and logical* queries can run, with a reasoning/AI layer operating *on top of* that structure rather than *instead of* it. The academic grounding here is decades old and well-proven — description logics, OWL, and entity-relationship modeling predate the current AI wave by 20+ years, and the current wave's contribution is making it far cheaper to *populate* an ontology from unstructured documents (via LLM extraction) — not a reason to discard the ontology itself in favor of pure retrieval.

## 2.2 What financial knowledge representation actually requires

**Entities.** Not just "invoice" and "vendor" as flat records — a mature financial ontology needs typed entities with inheritance: a Vendor is a specialization of Party, which is also specialized as Customer, Employee, or Related Party — and an entity can hold multiple roles simultaneously (a related party can also be a vendor). Flat, single-purpose tables (the norm in most ERP schemas) actively fight this requirement; an ontology layer is where the richer typing lives, independent of how the underlying ERP models it.

**Relationships.** Financial relationships are rarely binary. "Invoice belongs to Vendor" is binary; "this transaction is subject to related-party disclosure because the counterparty is a director's relative's firm" is an n-ary relationship depending on a chain of ownership/control facts that themselves need to be modeled as first-class relationships, not inferred ad hoc each time. Relationship types that must be modeled explicitly (not left implicit in text): ownership/control, related-party, debtor-creditor, tax-jurisdiction, statutory-filing-obligation, audit-trail (who-approved-what), and correction/supersession (this value replaced that value, because of this evidence).

**Temporal reasoning.** This is where generic knowledge-graph theory is least prepared for finance. A financial ontology must support **bitemporal modeling**: (1) valid time — when was this fact true in the real world (e.g., the GST rate that applied to a transaction on the transaction's date), and (2) transaction/system time — when did the system learn or record this fact (which may be much later, e.g., a correction entered today for a transaction from six months ago). Getting these two time dimensions confused is the single most common and most damaging modeling error in financial systems — it's the difference between "restate what we believed then" (valid time correction, requires reopening historical reports) and "record what we now know, going forward" (system time update only). An audit-grade ontology must keep both dimensions explicit and queryable independently.

**Entity resolution / identity resolution.** In real client data, the same vendor appears as "ABC Enterprises," "ABC Enterprises Pvt Ltd," "A.B.C. Enterprises" and possibly under multiple GSTINs for different branches/states of the same legal entity. Entity resolution — deciding which surface-form records refer to the same real-world entity — is a distinct, hard, well-studied problem (record linkage / deduplication literature going back decades in data-quality research) and is *not* solved by an LLM guessing string similarity; it requires a combination of deterministic keys (GSTIN, PAN — India's tax entity identifiers are actually unusually strong ground truth for this, better than most countries have), fuzzy matching as a secondary signal, and human confirmation for ambiguous cases, with the resolved identity itself becoming a first-class, versioned ontology node (so a later "we were wrong, these are actually two different entities" correction is possible and auditable, not a silent overwrite).

**Graph evolution.** An ontology is not static — new entity types, new relationship types, and schema changes happen as the domain (Part 4) expands (adding Payroll intelligence introduces Employee, PF-Account, ESI-Account as new entity types with new relationships to existing Vendor/Party nodes). The research lesson from every mature ontology-driven system (including Palantir's own public discussion of their ontology evolution practices) is that **schema migration must be versioned and backward-compatible by design from the start** — a graph that can't evolve without breaking every past query becomes a liability rather than an asset, and retrofitting versioned schema evolution onto a graph that wasn't designed for it is far more expensive than building it in from the first schema.

**Financial event modeling.** The correct unit of modeling is not the static record ("this invoice") but the **event** — an invoice being issued, a payment being made, a correction being applied, a voucher being pushed to Tally — each with an actor, a timestamp (in both temporal dimensions above), evidence, and consequences on the entity graph. This event-sourcing-adjacent view (well established in both distributed-systems literature and in double-entry-accounting theory itself, which has been event-sourced for 500+ years — a journal entry *is* an event) is the natural fit for finance specifically, more than for most other ontology domains, because accounting already *is* an immutable, append-only event log by convention (you don't edit a posted journal entry, you post a reversing entry) — the ontology should mirror that discipline rather than fight it with mutable state.

**Audit, compliance, accounting, tax, and business relationships as distinct relationship classes.** These should not be modeled as one generic "related to" edge type — each has different query patterns, different retention/versioning requirements, and different authority sources (accounting relationships are governed by Ind AS/AS, tax relationships by GST/Income Tax law, audit relationships by SA/ICAI standards, business relationships by commercial contracts). Collapsing them into one generic relationship type is a common shortcut that destroys the ontology's ability to answer domain-specific questions precisely (e.g., "show all related-party transactions requiring disclosure" needs to query a semantically distinct relationship class from "show all vendor payment relationships").

## 2.3 How financial reasoning must differ from generic knowledge-graph reasoning
Three concrete differences, each consequential for anything built later:
1. **Reasoning must be auditable, not just correct.** A generic knowledge graph optimizes for answer quality. A financial ontology must additionally produce, for every answer, the exact chain of facts and rules used — because the *proof* is often as legally important as the conclusion (an auditor doesn't just need "this is a related party," they need to show the ownership chain that establishes it).
2. **Reasoning must respect legal/statutory precedence, not just logical/statistical confidence.** A generic graph-reasoning system might resolve conflicting facts by confidence score; a financial ontology must resolve them by legal authority hierarchy (Part 1's conflict-resolution rules) — statistical confidence is not the correct arbiter when the actual arbiter is "which regulator's word is controlling here."
3. **Reasoning must be bitemporally correct by default**, because nearly every useful financial question ("was this compliant," "what was the balance," "who was liable") is implicitly date-scoped, whereas most generic knowledge-graph applications (recommendation, general Q&A) reason primarily in the present tense.

## 2.4 Why Palantir specifically built an ontology (and what transfers)
Palantir's own stated rationale (from their public technical writing and Foundry documentation) is that operational decision-making in complex organizations requires a *live, semantically typed model of the real world* that both humans and algorithms can act on with shared understanding — without it, every new AI/analytics use case has to independently re-solve "what does this data actually mean and how does it relate to everything else," which doesn't scale and doesn't compound. What transfers directly to a Company Brain for finance: the ontology as the **stable interface** between constantly-changing data sources/models and the applications built on top — models get swapped, data sources get added, but the ontology (accounting entities, tax relationships, audit trails) should change far more slowly and deliberately, acting as the load-bearing structure everything else attaches to. What does *not* transfer directly: Palantir's ontology is generically operational (any real-world entity in any industry); a financial ontology should be *narrower and deeper*, purpose-built around double-entry accounting's own event-sourced, bitemporal, statutorily-governed nature — which is a stronger and more specific structure than Palantir's general-purpose ontology needs to assume, and should be exploited rather than genericized away.

# PART 3 — KNOWLEDGE INGESTION RESEARCH

## 3.1 The core tension
Every serious enterprise-knowledge ingestion system (and the RAG-hallucination research literature broadly, see Part 9) converges on the same finding: **the failure mode is not usually "the model doesn't know enough" — it's "the model was given ingested content it should not have trusted, without a mechanism to know that."** Continuous ingestion without hallucination requires that trust and verification be engineered into the *ingestion pipeline itself*, not patched on at generation time via better prompting.

## 3.2 Source classes and how each must be handled differently

**Regulatory/statutory sources (GST, CBDT, ICAI, MCA, ROC, SEBI, RBI, budget documents, notifications, circulars, government APIs, case law).** These are authoritative but not machine-native — many arrive as PDFs or portal HTML with no stable schema, and their legal force depends on precise textual reading (a comma can matter). The correct ingestion posture: automated *fetching and change-detection* (has a new notification appeared, has an existing page changed) can and should be automated; **interpretation into a machine-usable rule (a specific rate, a specific threshold, a specific eligibility condition) must be human-verified before promotion into global knowledge**, because misreading a legal text is a liability event, not a quality-of-service degradation. Case law specifically deserves separate treatment — precedent doesn't mechanically expire the way a rate notification does, and its applicability is itself a judgment call, meaning case-law ingestion should feed a *reference/citable* layer for human review, not an auto-applied rule layer.

**Research papers, documentation, open source, GitHub.** Low-stakes relative to statutory sources — these inform system design and domain understanding (Part 9), not client-facing financial fact claims, and can be ingested with lighter-weight automated processes and lower trust-score ceilings; they should generally never be promoted into rule layers that directly produce a client-facing number.

**Internal company documents.** Firm-specific working papers, methodology notes, internal training material — this is where a CA firm's own accumulated judgment lives, and it is tenant-scoped (this firm's institutional knowledge, not shared globally) unless the firm explicitly opts to contribute a de-identified pattern to shared domain knowledge.

**Customer ERP data, invoices, bank statements, contracts, emails.** Highest-volume, most tenant-sensitive, and the primary fuel for episodic memory (Part 1). This is where entity resolution (Part 2) and extraction-correction-based learning (WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 3's loop) do the most work. The ingestion discipline here is strict tenant isolation from the moment of ingestion — not applied later at query time — plus the distinction, already established in Part 1, between raw ingested content (episodic, tenant-scoped) and any distilled pattern that might later be proposed for the shared domain-knowledge layer (which requires an explicit anonymization and validation step before it can cross that boundary).

**Policies and contracts.** Similar sensitivity to internal documents/ERP data; additionally these are often the *source of truth for relationship facts* in the ontology (a contract establishes a vendor relationship's terms, which the ontology should reference by provenance pointer rather than duplicate as free text).

## 3.3 Mechanisms required across all sources

**Versioning.** Every ingested item gets an immutable version id; re-ingestion of a changed source creates a new version with a supersession link, never an overwrite (Part 1's doctrine applied at ingestion).

**Validation.** Two-stage, matching Part 1: structural (schema conformance, automated, cheap, run on 100% of ingested items) then semantic (correctness, human-required for anything entering global/domain layers, sampled/risk-based for tenant-episodic data given its sheer volume — full human review of every invoice is not economically sensible and is exactly the problem AuditOS's extraction pipeline already exists to reduce, so the discipline here is to concentrate human review where uncertainty/impact is highest, using the extraction pipeline's own confidence signals to route review effort).

**Deduplication.** Distinct from entity resolution (Part 2) — this is about not ingesting the same source document twice (the codebase's existing MD5-based Drive-sync deduplication is the right primitive) — versus entity resolution's job of recognizing that two *different* documents refer to the *same* real-world entity.

**Conflict resolution and source ranking.** Applies Part 1's authority hierarchy at ingestion time: when two sources disagree, the ranking (specificity > recency-within-validity-window > authority) determines which becomes the trusted fact and which becomes a recorded-but-superseded/contested alternative — never silently discarded, because the losing source may become relevant again (e.g., a notification that was later struck down by a court, reviving the prior rule).

**Trust scoring.** Composed from source-class authority (statutory > professional-body > internal-firm > generic web), verification status, and corroboration count — and, per Part 1, must propagate to anything derived from the fact.

**Freshness and expiration.** Domain-specific windows (Part 4 details per-domain cadence) with the active-reverification-not-passive-staleness mechanism from Part 1.

**Human verification checkpoints.** Not a single generic "human in the loop" step — different checkpoints for different purposes: (1) *interpretation verification* for statutory sources (did we read this notification correctly), (2) *extraction verification* for client documents (did we extract this invoice correctly — the existing CA review workspace), (3) *promotion verification* for anything moving from tenant-episodic to shared domain knowledge (is this pattern real and safe to generalize), (4) *conflict adjudication* for cases Part 1's automatic resolution rules can't settle.

## 3.4 How world-class systems avoid ingestion-driven hallucination
The consistent finding across serious production RAG/knowledge-ingestion systems (and reflected in Anthropic's, Google's, and Microsoft's own published guidance on grounding and retrieval quality) is that hallucination from ingestion is minimized not by a smarter generation-time prompt but by **narrowing what the model is allowed to treat as ground truth at generation time to only verified, trust-scored, correctly-dated knowledge — and making the model's citation of that knowledge mandatory and checkable**, rather than optional. A model that must cite a specific, retrievable, versioned source for every factual claim it makes about tax rules is structurally far less able to hallucinate a rule than a model asked to "be careful and accurate." This validates, at the ingestion-and-retrieval layer, the same deterministic-guardrail philosophy WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md established at the output layer — grounding discipline has to run through the entire pipeline, not just be a final check.

# PART 4 — DOMAIN INTELLIGENCE RESEARCH (Ownership & Change Dynamics)

WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 4 mapped *why each domain matters* and *ingestion mode*. This section goes one layer deeper: **who owns the knowledge in the real world (which body has authority), and precisely how and how often it changes** — the operational detail needed before any ingestion cadence can be designed later.

| Domain | Real-world owner/authority | Change mechanism | Typical cadence | Learning mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GST | CBIC (central), State GST departments, GST Council | Notifications, circulars, GSTN portal/schema updates, GST Council meeting outcomes | Several times/year, occasionally mid-year with short compliance windows | Human-verified rule ingestion; portal schema tracked as versioned contract |
| Income Tax | CBDT, Finance Ministry | Union Budget (Finance Act), circulars, CBDT notifications | Annual major cycle (Budget), periodic circulars | Human-verified, assessment-year versioned |
| TDS/TCS | CBDT (part of Income Tax Act machinery) | Section/threshold amendments via Finance Act, occasional standalone notifications | Annual + occasional mid-year | Rule-table, effective-date versioned |
| Companies Act / MCA / ROC | Ministry of Corporate Affairs | Act amendments (rare), MCA rules/forms updates, portal version changes (e.g., V2→V3) | Infrequent for the Act itself; portal/form changes more often | Human-verified; low frequency, high precision required |
| ICAI / Auditing Standards | Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, NFRA for listed-entity oversight | Issuance/revision of Standards on Auditing, guidance notes | Slow, deliberative | Human-verified, treated as the methodology/eval bar, not a data feed |
| Accounting Standards (Ind AS/AS) | ICAI (AS), MCA/NFRA (Ind AS), converging toward IFRS (IASB influence) | Standard issuance/amendment, transition rules | Slow, multi-year transition windows | Human-verified, versioned with transition-period awareness |
| Payroll/PF/ESI | EPFO (PF), ESIC (ESI), state governments (Professional Tax) | Rate/threshold/wage-ceiling notifications, state-specific PT slabs | Periodic, state-fragmented for PT | Rule-table, state-and-date versioned |
| FEMA | RBI, Ministry of Finance | RBI Master Directions/circulars, FEMA rule amendments | Moderate, RBI-circular-driven | Human-verified, specialist domain |
| SEBI | SEBI | Regulations, circulars, board meeting outcomes | Frequent, high scrutiny | Human-verified, only relevant if serving listed/BFSI clients |
| RBI/Banking | RBI | Master Directions, circulars | Frequent | Human-verified, specialist |
| Risk/Fraud | No external regulatory owner — internally derived | Pattern evolution from aggregate transaction behavior | Continuous | Statistical/ML pattern-mining, not statute-based (this is the one domain in this table that is *learned*, not *ingested from an authority*) |
| Internal/Statutory/Cost Audit | ICAI (statutory/internal), Cost Accounting Standards Board / ICMAI (cost audit) | Standard issuance, applicability threshold changes | Slow | Human-verified methodology layer |
| Working Capital / Cash Flow / BI / CFO-CEO Advisory / Credit / Vendor / Benchmarking / Industry Intelligence | No single regulatory owner — derived analytically from the ontology plus aggregate cross-tenant patterns | Continuous, driven by the underlying transaction data itself | Continuous | Derived/analytical, built on the ontology (Part 2) once transactional trust is established — not separately "ingested" from an external authority, confirming WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md's sequencing point that advisory value depends on prior data trust |

**The operational insight this table adds beyond WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md:** domains split cleanly into two mechanically different categories — **authority-sourced domains** (GST through RBI/Banking: knowledge originates from an external body and must be ingested and human-verified) and **derived domains** (Risk/Fraud, and the entire Advisory cluster: knowledge originates from pattern-mining the organization's own accumulated ontology data). Conflating these two categories — trying to "ingest" a benchmark the way you ingest a GST notification, or trying to "derive" a tax rate the way you derive a cash-flow pattern — is a design error the ingestion pipeline (Part 3) must structurally prevent by routing each domain type through the correct mechanism from the start.

# PART 5 — SMALL LANGUAGE MODEL RESEARCH

## 5.1 The economic and scientific case for SLMs in this domain
The research literature on distillation (Hinton et al.'s foundational knowledge-distillation work and its many successors), on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA and QLoRA specifically), and on task-specific model performance all converge on a consistent, well-replicated finding: **for a narrow, well-specified, well-labeled task, a small fine-tuned model can match or exceed frontier-model accuracy at a fraction of the cost and latency, while a broad, novel, ambiguous task still favors frontier general reasoning.** This is not a hopeful prediction — it is already true today for tasks like classification, named-entity extraction, and structured-field-extraction when enough labeled data exists, and the gap only widens in SLMs' favor as distillation and fine-tuning tooling matures.

**LoRA/QLoRA** (low-rank adaptation, and its quantized variant) matter specifically because they make fine-tuning economically trivial relative to full fine-tuning — a small, well-curated, professionally-verified dataset (exactly what the WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 3 correction loop produces) can adapt an open-weight base model to a narrow financial task without the capital cost of full-parameter training, which is consistent with the Build-vs-Buy doctrine that AuditOS should never train a foundation model but *can*, once enough verified data exists, own lightweight adapters on top of one.

**RLHF vs. RLAIF.** RLHF (human feedback) is the gold standard for alignment quality but is expensive to collect at scale; RLAIF (AI-generated feedback, typically from a stronger model judging a weaker one) is cheaper but inherits and can amplify the judge model's own blind spots. For a domain with genuine liability consequences, the correct posture is that **RLHF-equivalent signal — actual professional (CA) correction/approval — is not optional and cannot be fully substituted by RLAIF**, because the "reward model" in RLAIF has no grounding in actual statutory correctness; it can only approximate what a strong LLM thinks looks right, which is a different and weaker guarantee than what a licensed professional confirmed is right. RLAIF may be useful for cheap, high-volume *pre-filtering* (catching obviously bad extractions before they reach a human), but the trusted training signal for anything promoted into the Company Brain's rule or semantic-memory layers must trace back to human professional judgment, per Part 1's ground-truth doctrine.

**Synthetic data.** Legitimate and valuable for robustness (format-variation stress-testing, adversarial edge cases) exactly as WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 3 already concluded — but synthetic data cannot establish correctness ground truth in a liability domain, because a synthetic example's "correct" label is itself generated by a model or a rule-set that could itself be wrong; it's useful for testing coverage, not for training final-authority behavior.

## 5.2 Evaluation
**Financial benchmarks.** Public benchmarks (e.g., FinQA-style numerical reasoning over financial documents, accounting-QA datasets) are useful for *directional* model selection (is this model family even competent at financial numerical reasoning) but are not sufficient for production trust decisions in this vertical, for the same reason Part 1 and WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md's Harvey analysis both establish: public benchmarks don't cover India-specific GST/TDS/Companies-Act correctness, and a model can score well on general financial-QA benchmarks while being wrong about India-specific statutory detail. **Reasoning benchmarks** (general logic/multi-step reasoning suites) are similarly a useful proxy for frontier-model *capability tier* selection, not a substitute for the domain-specific, professionally-graded eval set that is the actual moat (WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 3).

**Latency and cost** are not secondary concerns for SLM placement — they are often the *deciding* factor once accuracy parity is reached: a task performed thousands of times per client per month (line-item classification, ledger-name normalization) has a cost/latency profile that makes a frontier-model API call economically and operationally wrong even if the frontier model is marginally more accurate, whereas a task performed rarely per client (interpreting an ambiguous new invoice format never seen before) justifies frontier-model cost precisely because of its rarity and novelty.

**Deployment / edge inference.** Not yet a priority for this domain — financial data processing does not currently have a strong requirement for on-device/edge inference the way, say, mobile or IoT applications do; enterprise server-side deployment (with strict tenant isolation) is the appropriate deployment target for the foreseeable future. This may change if data-localization/on-premise requirements from regulated clients (BFSI, government-adjacent entities) become material — worth monitoring as a future constraint, not a current one.

## 5.3 Which workloads belong where
**Frontier models:** genuinely novel document formats never seen before; ambiguous classification requiring broad world knowledge and multi-step reasoning (is this expense plausible given the vendor's business and the client's industry); natural-language interaction with the CA reviewer; synthesizing a narrative explanation of a complex variance.
**SLMs (once sufficient verified data exists):** HSN/SAC code classification for known patterns; ledger-name normalization and entity resolution scoring; line-item field extraction for previously-seen invoice-format families; anomaly-score pre-filtering before human/frontier-model review; routine correction-pattern application (the "this vendor's invoices are always net-of-discount" class of learned rule, once verified, is often better encoded as a deterministic rule than even an SLM — see Part 1's procedural-memory doctrine — but the *classification of which rule applies* to a new instance is a good SLM task).

## 5.4 Can AuditOS eventually own domain SLMs — when, and why
Yes, and the "when" is data-gated, not time-gated: the precondition is a sufficient volume of **professionally-verified, correctly-labeled** examples for a specific narrow task (per Part 1's semantic-memory doctrine — not raw episodic volume, verified volume), because a fine-tuned model trained on unverified or noisy labels will confidently reproduce the noise. The "why" is threefold and consistent with WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md's moat analysis: (1) cost/latency economics at scale, (2) reduced dependency on any single frontier-model vendor for high-volume narrow tasks (resilience), and (3) — the deepest reason — a domain SLM trained on AuditOS's own verified-correction data *is itself an instantiation of the compounding moat*, portable to no competitor, because no competitor has the same verified correction history. This is the SLM strategy's actual strategic purpose: it's not primarily a cost optimization, it's a mechanism for converting the Company Brain's correction loop into an owned, difficult-to-replicate asset.

# PART 6 — AUTOMATION RESEARCH

## 6.1 The autonomous-workflow chain and where trust must gate it
The example chain given (Invoice → Extraction → Validation → Reconciliation → ERP → GST Filing → Email → Slack → Working Papers → Archive) is not a single automation to design — it is a sequence of distinct trust regimes, and research into production financial-automation failures (both in the RPA-era literature WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 1 already critiqued, and in more recent agentic-workflow research) converges on a consistent lesson: **failures cluster at the handoff points between systems of differing trust level, not within any single well-tested step.** The single most important research finding to carry forward is that automation reliability is a property of *how transitions between steps are gated and recovered*, not a property of how good any individual step's model is.

## 6.2 Human-in-the-loop as a designed control, not a fallback
The research distinction that matters: human-in-the-loop can mean (a) human reviews *before* an irreversible action (pre-execution gate — used for anything touching ERP writes or filings), (b) human reviews *after*, sampled, for quality assurance (post-hoc audit — appropriate for high-volume, low-individual-impact steps), or (c) human is only alerted on exception (management-by-exception — appropriate once a step has enough verified track record that full review is wasted effort). The research-backed principle: **the gate type must be chosen per step based on reversibility and blast radius of that specific step, not applied uniformly across the whole chain.** An ERP write or a GST filing is irreversible or costly-to-reverse and belongs at gate type (a); routing a working-paper file to archive storage is fully reversible and low-stakes and belongs at gate type (c). This mirrors, at the workflow-automation layer, the same reversibility-based judgment already applied to Claude's own action-taking policy in this very session — it is a domain-general principle for any consequential automation, not finance-specific, but finance-specific in *where the reversibility lines actually fall* (the GST-filing and ERP-write points, specifically).

## 6.3 Deterministic systems inside an otherwise probabilistic chain
Validation and Reconciliation, as named in the example chain, are exactly the steps WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md's permanent-deterministic-layer doctrine applies to — they must be deterministic-rule-driven with veto power over anything the (probabilistic) Extraction step produced, and this holds regardless of how good extraction models get, per that report's Part 2 conclusion. The research contribution to add here: **deterministic steps should also be the primary source of workflow-recovery signal** — because a deterministic validation failure has a precise, explainable cause (which rule failed, on which value), it is far better recovery-signal input than a probabilistic step's low-confidence score, which only says "something might be wrong" without saying what.

## 6.4 Exception handling and workflow recovery
Production-grade automation research (durable-execution and workflow-orchestration literature, e.g., the design principles behind systems like Temporal, and older sagas/compensating-transaction patterns from distributed systems) establishes that a multi-step chain touching external systems (ERP, government portals, communication channels) needs **idempotent steps and explicit compensating actions**, not just retry-until-success — because a naive retry after a partial failure (e.g., an ERP push that succeeded but whose success-confirmation was lost) can create duplicate financial records, which in an accounting context is a correctness-critical failure, not a minor annoyance. AuditOS's existing idempotent Tally-push-log pattern (WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 1) is already the correct application of this research-backed pattern; the principle to generalize is that **every external-system-touching step in the automation chain needs the same idempotency discipline**, not just the ERP-write step.

## 6.5 Observability and compliance logging as part of the automation, not bolted onto it
The research/industry consensus (validated by AuditBoard's core value proposition per WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 1, and by every serious workflow-orchestration system's design) is that observability for a financial automation chain must capture not just *system health* (did the step succeed, how long did it take — standard APM concerns) but **decision provenance** (what evidence and rule caused this specific automated decision) at every step, because the audit trail *is* the deliverable in this domain, not a debugging aid. This directly extends Part 1's provenance/lineage requirement from the knowledge layer into the workflow-execution layer — they are the same discipline applied to two different subsystems, and should be designed as one coherent capability, not two separate logging systems.

## 6.6 Risk controls
The relevant research area (financial-controls / SOX-methodology literature, and AuditBoard's core domain) establishes a standard taxonomy AuditOS's automation chain should be evaluated against: preventive controls (the pre-execution gate, 6.2's type (a)) vs. detective controls (post-hoc sampling/anomaly detection, MindBridge's full-population-testing philosophy from WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 1) vs. corrective controls (the compensating-action/recovery mechanisms in 6.4). A mature automated financial workflow needs all three types present and distinguishable in its design — a system with only preventive gates has no way to catch what slips through; a system with only detective controls after the fact has already let bad data reach the ERP/filing before catching it.

# PART 8 — OPEN SOURCE INTELLIGENCE

Evaluated on community health, maintenance activity, enterprise readiness, license posture, and scalability — with an explicit "when to adopt / when to avoid" for each category, since the category matters more here than any single named project (named projects will change faster than this document's shelf life; the category-level judgment should outlast specific product choices).

**OCR / Document AI.** Mature open-source options (e.g., Tesseract-lineage tools, layout-aware libraries like the one AuditOS already uses) are adequate for a *thin, controllable extraction layer* but generally trail commercial document-AI APIs (Google Document AI, AWS Textract, Azure Document Intelligence) on raw accuracy for complex financial-document layouts, and the commercial APIs improve faster because of far larger training investment. **Adopt open source when you need fine-grained control over the extraction process to feed domain-specific downstream logic** (which is AuditOS's actual reason for `pdfplumber`, per WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 5); **avoid relying on open source alone for raw accuracy at the frontier** — periodically benchmark against commercial APIs rather than assuming the open-source choice remains competitive indefinitely.

**Knowledge Graph.** Neo4j (source-available/commercial-core with a community edition) is the dominant, most enterprise-proven graph database; lighter alternatives exist as PostgreSQL extensions (e.g., graph-query layers over relational storage) which reduce operational surface area by not introducing a second database system. **Adopt a graph-native database once genuine multi-hop relational queries over the ontology (Part 2) become a real, frequent workload** (related-party chains, ownership traversal); **avoid adopting one prematurely** — a well-indexed relational schema can serve a young ontology's needs longer than teams expect, and operating a second database system has a real ongoing cost that should be paid for only when the graph-query workload justifies it.

**Agent frameworks.** The landscape (LangChain-style orchestration frameworks, and newer lighter alternatives) trades maintenance/community size against the indirection cost WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 5 already flagged. **Avoid heavy frameworks for the core deterministic-guardrail-wrapped-agent pattern** that is this domain's actual architecture — that pattern is simple enough to implement on thin, owned primitives, and a heavy framework's abstractions actively fight the requirement for full, auditable control over every step (Part 6's observability/provenance requirement is hard to guarantee through someone else's opaque orchestration layer). **Consider adopting a framework only for genuinely exploratory, low-stakes agent workflows** (internal tooling, research assistance) where auditability is not a hard requirement.

**Evaluation frameworks.** Open-source eval harnesses are useful *infrastructure* (running test suites, tracking regressions) but, per WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 3 and Part 5's Harvey lesson, **the eval dataset itself must be owned and built, never adopted from open source** — a generic financial-QA eval set doesn't test India-specific correctness and provides false confidence if treated as sufficient.

**Memory frameworks.** Emerging open-source "agent memory" libraries generally implement a single undifferentiated memory store, which Part 1 of this document establishes is a category error for finance (episodic/semantic/procedural need different retention, versioning, and trust-scoring treatment). **Adopt for the storage/retrieval plumbing** (vector search, basic session persistence) **but do not adopt their memory-taxonomy assumptions wholesale** — the taxonomy and governance rules in Part 1 must be imposed on top, regardless of which library provides the underlying storage primitive.

**Workflow engines.** As WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 5 already concluded, Celery (already in use) remains sufficient; heavier durable-execution engines (Temporal-class systems) become worth adopting specifically once the multi-step-with-compensating-actions requirement from Part 6.4 above grows complex enough that hand-rolling idempotency/recovery logic per-workflow becomes the larger maintenance burden — a threshold to monitor, not a current gap.

**RAG frameworks.** Useful for the mechanical plumbing (chunking, retrieval, reranking) but per Part 3's grounding-discipline conclusion, **the trust-scoring, versioning, and mandatory-citation requirements must be built as a layer on top of any RAG framework's default behavior**, since default open-source RAG implementations generally optimize for retrieval relevance, not for legal/provenance-grade groundedness.

**Ontology/OWL tooling.** Mature but academic-leaning (originating in semantic-web research); **adopt the conceptual discipline (typed entities, formal relationships, description-logic-style reasoning) without necessarily adopting heavyweight OWL tooling wholesale** — a lighter, purpose-built schema enforced at the application/database layer is usually more pragmatic for a fast-moving product than a full semantic-web stack, unless interoperability with external OWL-based systems becomes a real requirement.

**Vector databases.** As WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 5 already concluded, pgvector (given existing PostgreSQL usage) is the pragmatic default; dedicated vector databases (Qdrant, Weaviate, etc.) become worth the added operational system once vector-search scale/performance genuinely outgrows what a PostgreSQL extension can serve — again, a threshold to monitor, not adopt preemptively.

**Enterprise search.** Per WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 2's commoditization conclusion, no dedicated open-source search stack is worth adopting as a standalone capability; search should remain a thin layer over the ontology.

**MCP.** As an emerging *protocol* rather than a single library, the correct posture is to track its maturation and, per WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 2, consider exposing AuditOS's own reconciliation/ledger capabilities via an MCP-compatible interface once the protocol and its ecosystem (client support, security model for tool-calling in regulated domains) are mature enough to trust with real financial actions — this is a "when the ecosystem is ready" adoption, not a "build now" one, since MCP's security/authorization model for consequential financial actions is still actively evolving industry-wide as of this writing.

**General license/enterprise-readiness discipline.** For every category above, the recurring evaluation axis that matters most in this specific regulated domain (more than in a typical consumer-software adoption decision) is **whether the license and deployment model allow full self-hosting and full audit-log access** — a SaaS-only open-source-adjacent tool that doesn't allow AuditOS to inspect and retain its own provenance/logs at the level Part 1 and Part 6 require is not truly adoptable here, regardless of its technical merits, because it would create a black-box gap in exactly the chain that must be fully auditable.

# PART 9 — RESEARCH LITERATURE SYNTHESIS

Filtered strictly for durability — ideas likely to still be true in 2035, independent of which specific model or framework is fashionable at the time. Hype (specific benchmark leaderboard positions, specific model release comparisons, transient architecture fads) is deliberately excluded.

**On retrieval-augmented generation and grounding (broad literature since ~2020, reflected in Anthropic's, Google's, and Microsoft Research's published guidance on enterprise RAG).** The durable finding: grounding a model's output in retrievable, citable, versioned external knowledge reduces hallucination far more reliably than any amount of prompting or model-scale increase alone, and this holds regardless of how much frontier-model capability improves, because the failure mode being solved (unverifiable, undated, unretractable claims) is structural to trained-in weights, not a capability gap that scale fixes. This finding directly underwrites Part 1's and Part 3's core doctrine and should be treated as permanently true, not as a stopgap until "models get good enough."

**On knowledge distillation (Hinton et al. and the extensive lineage following it).** The durable finding: a smaller model trained to match a larger model's (or a verified ground-truth dataset's) behavior on a narrow task reliably captures most of the task-relevant capability at a fraction of the cost — this is a stable, well-replicated empirical result across a decade-plus of work, and underwrites Part 5's SLM strategy independent of which specific model families are current at any given time.

**On parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA and successors).** The durable finding: full-parameter retraining is rarely necessary to adapt a capable base model to a narrow domain task when working from a modest, high-quality labeled dataset — the cost/accuracy tradeoff strongly favors lightweight adaptation methods for exactly the kind of narrow, well-specified financial sub-tasks this domain generates. This is a durable engineering-economics finding, not a fashion.

**On knowledge graphs and neuro-symbolic reasoning (a research thread spanning classical AI, the semantic-web era, and the current renewed interest in combining LLMs with structured knowledge).** The durable finding, echoed consistently across decades of AI research including the field's own periodic "symbolic AI is dead / symbolic AI is back" cycles: purely statistical/neural systems are weak at precise, auditable, rule-governed, multi-hop relational reasoning, while purely symbolic systems are weak at handling messy, unstructured, natural-language input — the durable architecture is hybrid, with a neural layer for perception/language and a symbolic/structured layer for precise relational and rule-governed reasoning. This is not a temporary compromise pending better LLMs; the research literature's own history of oscillation between these two paradigms, always eventually converging back to hybrid approaches, is itself evidence that this is a structural property of the problem, not a capability gap.

**On document AI and layout-aware extraction (a steadily maturing applied-research area, e.g., work on layout-aware transformers for document understanding).** The durable finding: spatial/layout information is a first-class signal for document understanding, not an afterthought to be inferred from linearized text — validating AuditOS's own coordinate-aware extraction approach as aligned with the field's actual research direction, not merely an implementation convenience.

**On entity resolution / record linkage (a mature sub-field of data-quality research predating the current AI wave by decades, now being accelerated by LLM-assisted matching).** The durable finding: no purely automated method achieves the precision required for high-stakes identity decisions without a human-verification loop for ambiguous cases — this remains true even as LLM-assisted fuzzy matching improves the *automated proposal* quality, because the actual bottleneck is verifying correctness, not generating candidates.

**On enterprise AI trust and adoption (organizational/HCI research on AI-assisted decision-making, echoed in how Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft each frame enterprise AI trust publicly).** The durable finding: professionals in liability-bearing roles adopt and retain AI tools in proportion to the tool's *explainability and controllability*, not its raw capability — a more capable but less explainable tool is reliably trusted and adopted less than a less capable, fully explainable one, in every studied high-stakes professional domain (medicine, law, audit). This is the strongest possible research validation of WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md's central explainability thesis, and it comes from human-factors research independent of finance specifically, meaning it's a general property of professional trust in AI, not a finance-specific quirk that might fade.

**On audit AI and anomaly detection specifically (applied research adjacent to MindBridge's own domain).** The durable finding: full-population anomaly scoring with explainable, transaction-level risk drivers outperforms both pure statistical sampling (misses population-wide patterns) and pure black-box ML scoring (unusable by professionals who must justify findings) — reinforcing WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md Part 1's MindBridge lesson from the research-literature side, not just the company-case-study side.

**What to explicitly ignore as hype rather than durable signal.** Any specific claim of the form "model X now beats human experts on benchmark Y" should be read as a capability-tier data point only, never as license to remove deterministic guardrails or human verification checkpoints — per the human-factors research above, the adoption/trust bottleneck in this domain is not primarily a capability ceiling, so raw benchmark improvements do not on their own change any of the doctrine in this report. Similarly, any framework or tool whose primary claim to relevance is "an agent orchestration paradigm" without addressing auditability, provenance, or bitemporal correctness is solving a different problem than the one this domain has.

# PART 10 — COMPANY BRAIN PRINCIPLES (Permanent Doctrine)

These are designed to hold regardless of which model generation, framework, or specific competitor is current — tested against every finding in Parts 1-9 and WORLD_INTELLIGENCE_REPORT.md. They are organized by theme, not priority; all are permanent.

**On knowledge and memory**
1. **Knowledge First.** The ontology and verified knowledge base outlive every model version; models are interchangeable reasoning components over durable knowledge, never the other way around.
2. **Memory Has Types, Not One Bucket.** Episodic, semantic, and procedural memory have different retention, versioning, and trust rules; collapsing them into one store is a standing architectural error.
3. **Trained-In Knowledge Is Never Authoritative.** Anything a model "knows" from training weights, rather than from a versioned retrievable source, must never be treated as ground truth for a client-facing financial fact.
4. **Every Fact Has a Lifespan.** Nothing is stored without an effective-from date, and nothing is presumed current without an active freshness check appropriate to its domain.
5. **Supersede, Never Erase.** Obsolete knowledge is marked superseded with a pointer to what replaced it and why; true deletion is reserved only for data that was never trusted in the first place.
6. **Bitemporal by Default.** Every fact and every derived conclusion distinguishes when it was true in the world from when the system learned it; conflating the two is treated as a correctness bug, not a modeling nuance.

**On trust and verification**
7. **Ground Truth Is Professional Judgment, Not Model Confidence.** The only signal that promotes a pattern into trusted semantic memory or a fine-tuned model is verified human professional approval, not statistical confidence, however high.
8. **Trust Propagates and Degrades.** A conclusion is never more trustworthy than its least trustworthy input; trust scores flow downstream and cap accordingly.
9. **Unresolved Conflicts Surface, They Don't Get Silently Resolved.** When authority-hierarchy rules can't settle a disagreement between sources, a human adjudicates — the system never guesses and presents the guess as fact.
10. **Verification Checkpoints Are Differentiated by Purpose.** Interpretation-verification, extraction-verification, promotion-verification, and conflict-adjudication are distinct checkpoints; a single generic "human review" step is insufficient.
11. **Structural Validation Is Automated; Semantic Validation Is Not.** Schema conformance can run on every ingested item automatically; correctness for anything entering shared knowledge requires a human, without exception.

**On reasoning**
12. **Deterministic Before Probabilistic, Permanently.** Anything with exactly one correct answer (tax math, debit/credit balance, statutory apportionment) is computed by rules with veto power over any model output, forever — not a maturity stage the system graduates out of.
13. **Reasoning Must Be Provable, Not Just Correct.** Every conclusion the system produces must be reconstructible as an explicit chain of facts and rules, on demand, for a skeptical human.
14. **Legal Authority Outranks Statistical Confidence.** When resolving which of two conflicting facts to trust, the hierarchy is regulatory authority and specificity, never a confidence score.
15. **Match the Reasoning Mode to the Question.** Rule execution, grounded retrieval, and open-ended model judgment are three distinct tools; routing a question to the wrong one is treated as a design defect, not a stylistic choice.

**On the ontology**
16. **The Ontology Is the Stable Interface.** Data sources and models change constantly; the entity/relationship model they operate over changes slowly and deliberately, and everything else is built to depend on its stability.
17. **Model Relationships as First-Class, Typed, Multiple.** A single entity may hold multiple roles (vendor, related party) simultaneously; relationships are typed and distinguished by legal/domain class, never collapsed into one generic edge.
18. **Financial Events, Not Static Records, Are the Unit of Truth.** Every state change is modeled as an evidenced, actored, timestamped event, mirroring double-entry accounting's own five-century-old event-sourced discipline rather than fighting it with mutable records.
19. **Identity Resolution Requires Deterministic Anchors Plus Human Confirmation.** GSTIN/PAN-grade identifiers anchor entity resolution; fuzzy matching proposes, a human confirms ambiguous cases, and the resolved identity itself is versioned and revisable.
20. **Schema Evolves Without Breaking History.** New entity and relationship types are added through versioned, backward-compatible migration, never through a redesign that orphans past reasoning.

**On learning and evaluation**
21. **The Eval Set Is a Strategic Asset, Not a QA Task.** A professionally-graded, India-specific, continuously-growing evaluation set is owned and invested in as deliberately as any product feature — it is what makes trust claims to regulators and institute bodies possible.
22. **Learn Continuously, Promote Deliberately.** Every correction is captured immediately; promotion of a pattern into a trusted rule or a training example goes through the same verification discipline as any other knowledge entering global layers.
23. **Synthetic Data Tests Coverage, Never Establishes Truth.** Synthetic examples stress-test robustness; they never become the ground-truth label for a liability-bearing claim.
24. **SLMs Are a Byproduct of the Correction Loop, Not a Parallel Project.** A domain SLM is only built once sufficient verified data exists to train it correctly — data-gated, not calendar-gated.
25. **Benchmarks Inform Capability Tier, Not Production Trust.** Public and general benchmarks help choose which frontier model to route a task to; they never substitute for the owned, domain-specific eval set when deciding what ships.

**On customer and organizational boundaries**
26. **Tenant Isolation at Rest, Not Just at Retrieval.** Client data never comingles into shared knowledge layers at the storage level; filtering at query time is not sufficient if raw isolation was ever violated upstream.
27. **Aggregate Learning, Never Raw Data Sharing.** Cross-client intelligence (risk patterns, benchmarks) is learned in de-identified aggregate and must be provably explainable as such to a skeptical client's own compliance reviewer.
28. **Firm Institutional Knowledge Stays With the Firm Unless Explicitly Shared.** A CA firm's own working methodology and internal notes are tenant-scoped by default; contribution to shared domain knowledge is opt-in, not automatic.

**On explainability and provenance**
29. **Every Output Traces to Source.** From a final reconciled number back to the specific document region, rule, and reviewer decision that produced it — no exceptions, no "trust the model" endpoints.
30. **Provenance and Observability Are One Discipline, Not Two Systems.** Decision provenance in the knowledge layer and execution observability in the workflow layer are designed as a single coherent capability.
31. **Explainability Is a Permanent Requirement, Not a Maturity-Stage Crutch.** As models improve, the requirement to explain their output does not relax — professional trust research shows explainability drives adoption independent of raw capability, permanently.

**On automation and workflow**
32. **Gate by Reversibility, Not Uniformly.** Every automated step's human-review posture (pre-execution gate, post-hoc sample, exception-only) is chosen by that specific step's reversibility and blast radius, not applied as one policy across an entire chain.
33. **Idempotency Is Required at Every External-System Boundary.** Any step touching an ERP, a government portal, or a communication channel is built to be safely retryable without duplication, as a standing requirement, not a per-incident fix.
34. **Preventive, Detective, and Corrective Controls All Coexist.** No automated financial workflow is considered complete with only one control type present.

**On infrastructure and build discipline**
35. **Build the Ontology, the Rules, the Connectors to the Ledger, and the Eval Loop — Buy or API Everything Else.** This line is re-tested against every new capability before a build decision is made, permanently, not reassessed for convenience.
36. **Commodity Infrastructure Is Never a Differentiator, However Well Built.** Investing engineering time to out-build a well-funded infrastructure vendor at their own commodity layer is treated as a standing anti-pattern, not judged case-by-case on enthusiasm.
37. **Open Source Is Adopted for Control, Not Cost Alone.** The deciding factor for adopting an open-source component in this domain is whether it preserves full audit-log and provenance access, not merely whether it's free or popular.

**On organizational humility toward the domain**
38. **India-Specific Correctness Is Earned, Never Assumed From General Capability.** A model or benchmark being strong in general financial reasoning is never sufficient evidence of GST/TDS/Companies-Act-specific correctness; that must be separately, continuously verified.
39. **Authority-Sourced Knowledge and Derived Knowledge Are Never Confused.** Statutory facts are ingested and human-verified from an external authority; advisory/benchmark insights are derived analytically from the ontology — routing one through the other's mechanism is treated as a category error.
40. **Case Law and Interpretive Guidance Inform Judgment; They Do Not Auto-Apply.** Precedent and guidance feed the human-verification layer, never the auto-applied rule layer, because their applicability is itself a judgment call the system does not have standing to make alone.

**On long-term posture**
41. **The System Must Be Able to Reproduce Any Past Decision Under the Rules That Applied Then.** Historical reproducibility under period-correct rules is treated as a hard requirement, tested continuously, not an aspiration.
42. **Liability Sits With the Human, By Design, Permanently.** The system is architected so a licensed professional remains the accountable decision-maker at every consequential step — this is a design constraint the system enforces structurally, not a disclaimer in the terms of service.
43. **A Wrong "I Don't Know" Is Always Cheaper Than a Confident Wrong Answer.** Every ambiguous case defaults to surfacing uncertainty to a human rather than resolving it silently, even at the cost of throughput.
44. **The Company Brain Is a Loop, Not an Artifact.** It has no finished state — correction, verification, promotion, and re-evaluation run continuously for as long as the company exists, and its value is precisely that this loop compounds and cannot be shortcut by a competitor arriving later.
45. **Every Principle Above Is Falsifiable and Must Be Re-Tested, Not Just Followed.** These are treated as the current best understanding of what a decade-durable financial intelligence company requires, subject to revision if evidence — not fashion, not competitive pressure, not model-capability hype — shows a principle to be wrong.
